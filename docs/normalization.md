# Loudness normalization (2.9)

The feature is disabled by default. Each platform stores its own boolean under `tm-yt-volume-slider-normalization` or `tm-twitch-volume-slider-normalization`. The settings controller keeps an unsaved session choice when storage is blocked. The main slider mode must also be enabled. This feature adds no userscript grants or runtime dependencies.

## Audio path and intent

`src/shared/normalization.js` captures a copy using `video.captureStream()` (or `mozCaptureStream`). After the processor module loads, `lufs-analysis.js` creates a `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode` connected to an AudioWorklet with zero outputs. A temporary Blob URL loads the bundled processor once per audio context and is revoked after loading. If the worklet cannot load or is unsupported, a browser with audio `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` support reads the captured track's `AudioData` directly instead, without creating an unused Web Audio input graph. Both paths use the same sample-clocked meter from `lufs-meter.js`, with stereo speaker downmix including mono duplication. The meter reports 50 ms energy frames without retaining a recording. Only three seconds of energy history are kept; direct audio frames close immediately after processing and the reader requests a one-frame buffer. Channel buffers and input views are reused until the frame size or channel layout changes.

LUFS sums K-weighted playback-channel energies separately, avoiding cancellation between opposite-phase stereo channels. The same filtered samples also supply approximately 5 ms energy measurements for large-spike protection; this short energy estimate is not a separate LUFS meter or normalization method. Reporting is adaptive: a rapid rise enables 5 ms reports for 100 ms, while steady audio retains approximately 20 ms averaged reports. The filters process each channel in blocks ending at measurement boundaries, keeping filter state in local scalars between boundaries. No splitter, polling analysers, or alternate normalization engine are allocated. Direct frame processing runs on the page thread when the worklet path is unavailable; it does not change the page's security policy. Audio support in `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` varies between browsers.

Captured video tracks stop immediately. Analysis never connects to the audio destination and never creates a `MediaElementAudioSourceNode`. The site's original playback path remains intact if capture fails, including cross-origin restrictions or protected content. Capture follows the [W3C media capture contract](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-fromelement/): captured audio is independent of the element's volume and mute, so native volume adjustments do not form a feedback loop. There is no microphone access, audio recording, upload, or second audible playback path.

The platform adapter exposes raw volume setters/getters to the normalizer. Its existing slider, persistence, startup restoration, and mute logic use the preferred volume instead. The controller recognizes its own rounded output and maps it back to preferred intent. Native volume changes that differ from the last applied output become new intent. Synchronous notifications between an API's unmute and volume update are guarded so they cannot overwrite that intent.

Because adjustment uses native player volume, the site's own volume indicator can move while the custom slider stays at the preferred level. Output is never amplified beyond the native player's range. No normalization setting can make a player at 100% louder than its original source.

While active, General → Audio shows whether the controller is raising quiet audio or reducing loud audio on one line, followed by a compact comparison such as “Output 17% · Selected 30%”. The deliberate line break keeps the percentages together at normal menu widths. This detail refreshes at most twice per second and is excluded from live announcements; status changes remain accessible. It exposes corrections without moving the custom slider.

A small, static SVG waveform icon tucked into the volume ring's upper-right edge appears only while normalization is Active, including at the volume limit. It hides during startup, pause, mute, activation waits, inaccessible or unavailable audio, and when normalization is off. Quiet audio and silence with healthy capture remain Active. It slightly overlaps the outer band of the arc, keeping the volume number and most of the ring unobscured. The 12×12 CSS-pixel icon leaves the original layout and circular background unchanged and adds no text, animation, font measurement, or timer. Status changes update visibility and its accessible description; output-only updates leave its DOM untouched. The icon uses each platform's accent and works in New and Classic appearances and at on-video sizes. Audio settings show status and adjusted output.

Active status follows capture readiness, not measured signal energy. Continuous quiet audio crossing the noise floor, speech gaps, and silence keep the capture Active while gain holds as needed. Only playback, context, or capture availability changes produce waiting or unavailable statuses. The loudness tracker has no separate public audibility state for the UI to synchronize.

## Leveling policy

- Apply [BS.1770 K weighting](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bs/R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf) with sample-rate-aware filters and sum channel energies. Process continuous captured samples rather than polling short analyser snapshots. The meter exposes ungated 400 ms momentary and three-second short-term readings, following [EBU Tech 3341](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3341.pdf). Tests cover its stereo reference levels at 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz; this is not a claim of full EBU meter certification.
- The separate live gain controller uses the last three seconds of active K-weighted energy. It excludes frames more than 10 dB below that window's average so quiet word endings do not continually lower the estimate. After one second of measured audio, it also remembers the established reference through distinctly quieter backgrounds. When the current frame is more than 10 LU below that reference, the held reference falls by at most 0.5 LU per second of measured audio; ordinary programme frames resume rolling tracking. Silence holds this memory, and a source reset discards it. This prevents background audio outlasting the three-second window from quickly building a large boost before speech returns. This gated control estimate is not a whole-programme integrated LUFS measurement.
- Target a source reference of −18 LUFS before the user's volume. This preserves a similar steady-tone listening level to the previous controller while improving spectral matching. A selected 30% gives a nominal −28.5 LUFS target, and 40% gives −26 LUFS (`−18 + 20 log10(selected / 100)`). It is a digital reference, not a calibrated acoustic dB level or a guarantee for dynamic material. This choice is separate from EBU broadcast delivery targets.
- Allow up to 20 dB of attenuation and 6 dB of automatic boost, bounded further by the native player's remaining 0–100% range. Limiting the gain already present during quiet audio reduces the beginning of a sudden louder sound before a reactive measurement can arrive. Especially quiet recordings can remain below target. A positive preference never rounds into an automatic mute; native percentage rounding can slightly exceed the nominal boost limit.
- Apply programme-level decreases immediately; smooth increases in decibels with a 900 ms time constant during the first three seconds and a three-second time constant afterward. Quiet gaps do not restart the faster startup ramp. Require one second of measurable audio before raising volume.
- Keep temporary loud-event protection separate from programme matching. A 400 ms estimate limits louder passages; a 50 ms frame more than 6 LU above the active reference bypasses that window for a cut. The adaptive short K-weighted estimate can cut sooner when the programme-corrected source exceeds the target by more than 10 LU. This fast protection remains armed for 100 ms of programme time to finish measuring a spike that starts partway through a block. It can only lower gain and does not enter the programme history. During recovery, attenuation below unity releases with a 250 ms time constant; amplification above unity rebuilds with a 900 ms time constant. These portions interpolate separately in decibels, with no abrupt behavior change at unity. Both remain bounded by the programme correction, whose upward time constant is still three seconds after startup. Apply reductions as measurements arrive instead of waiting for the output timer; gradual increases retain the 50 ms timer. Benign short measurements skip player volume/mute API reads, while ordinary frames and the timer continue tracking native intent. The 400 ms/three-second LUFS meter windows remain unchanged.
- Freeze below raw channel RMS 0.01 (about −40 dBFS), through pause, and during mute/zero volume. This does not lift silence or steady very low noise.

This is live LUFS-based leveling, not advance normalization of an entire recording or a true-peak limiter. Measurement, message delivery, timer scheduling, and native percentage rounding all affect the result. It cannot anticipate the first loud moment, remove every short peak, overcome the native volume limit, or guarantee identical subjective loudness. Device volume and the operating system's audio processing remain outside its control.

The 5 ms analysis interval is not a speaker-output latency guarantee. Native audio frames may arrive in larger packets, and a busy page delays native-volume writes on either path. Retaining the established speech reference reduces the boost already present before a word starts, but it cannot anticipate an unknown source's first loud word. Very long quiet passages can eventually regain boost, and extremely brief clicks can finish before a reactive correction takes effect. Eliminating that limit would require changing the playback path and adding look-ahead, with different compatibility and latency tradeoffs.

## Lifecycle and fallback

One enabled player owns one polling timer, capture stream, and audio context. Repeated binding to the same video is idempotent. Track additions are handled by capture events; the timer scans again when the existing audio track ends or no track is available, without enumerating stable track lists on every tick. Source load events discard the old capture, restore preferred volume, and restart measurement; new audio tracks replace the analysis source. Switching off or disposing the overlay removes listeners, stops captured tracks, disconnects nodes, closes the worklet port or cancels the native frame reader, closes the context, and restores preferred volume with mute preserved. A pending worklet load cannot attach either processor after disposal, and a native frame already being processed cannot send further callbacks after its owner is disposed. A detached video's API is not used for restoration because it may already point at an incoming player.

LUFS is the only normalization method. The selected volume remains unchanged while the processor starts, with a Starting audio analysis status. A blocked or missing worklet tries native frame access before declaring analysis unavailable. If neither input path can start, processing fails, no initial measurement arrives within three seconds of active playback, or measurements stop for over 500 ms after starting, the controller restores ordinary volume, releases capture, and reports normalization as unavailable. If the polling timer itself was delayed by over 500 ms, it holds the correction for one additional 100 ms catch-up period so queued audio can run first. Without a fresh frame, that grace cannot restart. Pause, mute, worklet activation waits, and inaccessible tracks do not consume the startup grace period. Native frame startup and stall timeouts apply even while its unused context is suspended. Playback or metadata events, or toggling normalization off and on, can retry a failed connection.

Missing capture APIs, encrypted content, capture exceptions, and muted/unavailable capture tracks have visible status messages. Exceptions and inaccessible tracks restore ordinary volume. The input adapter reports whether it needs context activation: worklet analysis waits for a running context and can resume on a pointer or keyboard gesture; native frame analysis does not use that graph and runs even while its unused context is suspended. It needs no extra activation click once playback provides accessible frames. Mute, pause, source reset, frame timeouts and cleanup still apply to either adapter.

Capture and processing failures, including frame timeouts, release the failed analysis and retry on the existing output timer after 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then at most every 30 seconds of wall time. Retries wait while playback is paused, ended, or not ready. Three seconds of healthy analysis reset the delay; source resets also discard backoff. A pointer/keyboard gesture or playback/metadata event can retry immediately. Unsupported capture APIs and protected media do not retry on the timer. Switching off or detaching cancels pending recovery with the session, and ordinary playback continues at the selected volume while unavailable.

## Verification

`npm run check` covers the LUFS leveling policy and both generated userscripts, including opt-in persistence, blocked storage, user intent, synchronous player events, mode Off, mute, source changes, failure recovery, and cleanup. Meter tests cover reference levels, sample rates, chunk boundaries, phase, channel weights, window startup, and silence. Gain tests cover quiet/loud sources at 30% and 40%, bounded quiet boost and native headroom at 20–100%, speech gaps, first-frame cuts, and recovery after a transient. Both standalone scripts also exercise quiet startup at a selected 20%, its rounded 40% output bound, a loud return and restoration when disabled. Worklet lifecycle tests cover module reuse, loading failures, disposal during loading, processor errors, startup/stalled-frame timeouts, and frozen gain during mute/pause. Artifact assertions exclude the former RMS controller and analyser graph from both bundles. Status regressions keep a single capture connected through continuous low-level audio and silence without repeated Active/waiting transitions.

`npm run test:browser` also checks the static icon with a controlled analysis fixture: original control dimensions, both appearances, on-video scaling, active-only visibility, accessible descriptions, and removal during mute, inaccessible capture, zero volume, and disable. It verifies placement at ten page zoom levels from 50% to 200% and unobscured selected values through 100%. Hidden states preserve the artwork for recovery. Set `VOLUME_SLIDER_SCREENSHOTS` to save icon and zoom screenshots.

`npm run test:normalization-browser` uses installed Chrome or Edge with synthetic WAV audio and local routes on the platform origins. It verifies actual LUFS worklet frames with no output connections, quiet/loud matching at 30% and 40%, available native headroom, opposite-phase stereo, frequency-dependent correction, stable slider/storage preference, mute preservation, capture cleanup, and ordinary playback after cross-origin capture fails. A same-source quiet-to-loud transition measures the time until native volume remains within 2 dB of target, with a 250 ms regression ceiling. This measures capture-to-player correction, not latency at the speakers. Pure tests sweep 50 spike-onset phases and require the large-spike correction within 15 ms of sample time. Other regressions cover adaptive reporting, multichannel chunk independence at 44.1/48/96 kHz, stable-track scan suppression, native-frame disposal, and skipped player reads for benign short measurements. The same browser behavior is exercised with a real Content Security Policy blocking Blob worklet modules and with AudioWorklet removed, verifying native frame processing. With both input APIs absent, playback continues at the selected volume with unavailable status. It never contacts YouTube or Twitch. Set `VOLUME_SLIDER_CHROME` to choose a browser and optionally `VOLUME_SLIDER_SCREENSHOTS` to save options screenshots.

`npm run test:normalization-compare` uses optional FFmpeg reference metering and an optional attributed speech/music corpus. It also processes 30 minutes of sample-clocked audio at 96 kHz without retaining audio or frame history beyond the bounded meter/controller windows. See [testing](testing.md#loudness-comparison) for the method, audio attribution, and reproduction instructions.

These tests do not establish compatibility with every live player version, Twitch stream type, ad transition, or browser. Measured consistency does not replace subjective listening across real content.
