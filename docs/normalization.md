# Loudness normalization (2.9)

The feature is disabled by default. Each platform stores its own boolean under `tm-yt-volume-slider-normalization` or `tm-twitch-volume-slider-normalization`. The settings controller keeps an unsaved session choice when storage is blocked. The main slider mode must also be enabled. This feature adds no userscript grants or runtime dependencies.

## Audio path and intent

`src/shared/normalization.js` captures a copy using `video.captureStream()` (or `mozCaptureStream`). `lufs-analysis.js` normally connects its `MediaStreamAudioSourceNode` to an AudioWorklet with zero outputs. A temporary Blob URL loads the bundled processor once per audio context and is revoked after loading. If the worklet cannot load or is unsupported, a browser with audio `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` support reads the captured track's `AudioData` directly instead. Both paths use the same sample-clocked meter from `lufs-meter.js`, with stereo speaker downmix including mono duplication. The meter reports 50 ms energy frames without retaining a recording. Only three seconds of energy history are kept; direct audio frames close immediately after processing and the reader buffers at most one pending frame.

LUFS sums K-weighted playback-channel energies separately, avoiding cancellation between opposite-phase stereo channels. No splitter, polling analysers, or alternate normalization engine are allocated. Direct frame processing runs on the page thread when the worklet path is unavailable; it does not change the page's security policy. Audio support in `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` varies between browsers.

Captured video tracks stop immediately. Analysis never connects to the audio destination and never creates a `MediaElementAudioSourceNode`. The site's original playback path remains intact if capture fails, including cross-origin restrictions or protected content. Capture follows the [W3C media capture contract](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-fromelement/): captured audio is independent of the element's volume and mute, so native volume adjustments do not form a feedback loop. There is no microphone access, audio recording, upload, or second audible playback path.

The platform adapter exposes raw volume setters/getters to the normalizer. Its existing slider, persistence, startup restoration, and mute logic use the preferred volume instead. The controller recognizes its own rounded output and maps it back to preferred intent. Native volume changes that differ from the last applied output become new intent. Synchronous notifications between an API's unmute and volume update are guarded so they cannot overwrite that intent.

Because adjustment uses native player volume, the site's own volume indicator can move while the custom slider stays at the preferred level. Output is never amplified beyond the native player's range. No normalization setting can make a player at 100% louder than its original source.

While active, General → Audio shows whether the controller is raising quiet audio or reducing loud audio on one line, followed by a compact comparison such as “Output 17% · Selected 30%”. The deliberate line break keeps the percentages together at normal menu widths. This detail refreshes at most twice per second and is excluded from live announcements; status changes remain accessible. It exposes corrections without moving the custom slider.

A small, static SVG waveform icon tucked into the volume ring's upper-right edge indicates that normalization is enabled. It slightly overlaps the outer band of the arc, keeping the volume number and most of the ring unobscured. It represents the saved option, including during mute or when analysis is waiting or unavailable; Audio settings show the actual status and adjusted output. The 12×12 CSS-pixel icon leaves the original layout and circular background unchanged and adds no text, animation, font measurement, timer, or meter updates. A tooltip and accessible description explain its meaning without adding a tab stop or live announcements. The icon uses each platform's accent, works in New and Classic appearances and at on-video sizes, and hides when normalization is off.

Active status follows capture readiness, not measured signal energy. Continuous quiet audio crossing the noise floor, speech gaps, and silence keep the capture Active while gain holds as needed. Only playback, context, or capture availability changes produce waiting or unavailable statuses. The loudness tracker has no separate public audibility state for the UI to synchronize.

## Leveling policy

- Apply [BS.1770 K weighting](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bs/R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf) with sample-rate-aware filters and sum channel energies. Process continuous captured samples rather than polling short analyser snapshots. The meter exposes ungated 400 ms momentary and three-second short-term readings, following [EBU Tech 3341](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3341.pdf). Tests cover its stereo reference levels at 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz; this is not a claim of full EBU meter certification.
- The separate live gain controller uses the last three seconds of active K-weighted energy. It excludes frames more than 10 dB below that window's average so quiet word endings do not continually lower the estimate. This gated, rolling control estimate is not a whole-programme integrated LUFS measurement.
- Target a source reference of −18 LUFS before the user's volume. This preserves a similar steady-tone listening level to the previous controller while improving spectral matching. A selected 30% gives a nominal −28.5 LUFS target, and 40% gives −26 LUFS (`−18 + 20 log10(selected / 100)`). It is a digital reference, not a calibrated acoustic dB level or a guarantee for dynamic material. This choice is separate from EBU broadcast delivery targets.
- Allow up to 20 dB of attenuation and as much increase as the native player's remaining 0–100% range permits. A positive preference never rounds into an automatic mute.
- Apply programme-level decreases immediately; smooth increases in decibels with a 900 ms time constant during the first three seconds and a three-second time constant afterward. Quiet gaps do not restart the faster startup ramp. Require one second of measurable audio before raising volume.
- Keep temporary loud-event protection separate from programme matching. A 400 ms estimate limits louder passages; a 50 ms frame more than 6 LU above the active reference bypasses that window for an immediate cut. Temporary attenuation releases with a 250 ms time constant, bounded by the programme correction, so a transient does not leave normal speech at its peak-derived gain for several seconds. Native output updates remain on the 50 ms main-thread timer.
- Freeze below raw channel RMS 0.01 (about −40 dBFS), through pause, and during mute/zero volume. This does not lift silence or steady very low noise.

This is live LUFS-based leveling, not advance normalization of an entire recording or a true-peak limiter. Measurement, message delivery, timer scheduling, and native percentage rounding all affect the result. It cannot anticipate the first loud moment, remove every short peak, overcome the native volume limit, or guarantee identical subjective loudness. Device volume and the operating system's audio processing remain outside its control.

## Lifecycle and fallback

One enabled player owns one polling timer, capture stream, and audio context. Repeated binding to the same video is idempotent. Source load events discard the old capture, restore preferred volume, and restart measurement; new audio tracks replace the analysis source. Switching off or disposing the overlay removes listeners, stops captured tracks, disconnects nodes, closes the worklet port or cancels the native frame reader, closes the context, and restores preferred volume with mute preserved. A pending worklet load cannot attach either processor after disposal. A detached video's API is not used for restoration because it may already point at an incoming player.

LUFS is the only normalization method. The selected volume remains unchanged while the processor starts, with a Starting audio analysis status. A blocked or missing worklet tries native frame access before declaring analysis unavailable. If neither input path can start, processing fails, no initial measurement arrives within three seconds of active playback, or measurements stop for over 500 ms after starting, the controller restores ordinary volume, releases capture, and reports normalization as unavailable. Pause, mute, suspended contexts, and inaccessible tracks do not consume the startup grace period. Playback or metadata events, or toggling normalization off and on, can retry a failed connection.

Missing capture APIs, encrypted content, capture exceptions, suspended contexts, and muted/unavailable capture tracks have visible status messages. Exceptions and inaccessible tracks restore ordinary volume. A suspended context can resume on a pointer or keyboard gesture. Capture exceptions retry on playback or metadata events, rather than on every polling tick.

## Verification

`npm run check` covers the LUFS leveling policy and both generated userscripts, including opt-in persistence, blocked storage, user intent, synchronous player events, mode Off, mute, source changes, failure recovery, and cleanup. Meter tests cover reference levels, sample rates, chunk boundaries, phase, channel weights, window startup, and silence. Gain tests cover quiet/loud sources at 30% and 40%, speech gaps, first-frame cuts, and recovery after a transient. Worklet lifecycle tests cover module reuse, loading failures, disposal during loading, processor errors, startup/stalled-frame timeouts, and frozen gain during mute/pause. Artifact assertions exclude the former RMS controller and analyser graph from both bundles. Status regressions keep a single capture connected through continuous low-level audio and silence without repeated Active/waiting transitions.

`npm run test:browser` also checks the static icon with a controlled analysis fixture: original control dimensions, both appearances, on-video scaling, mute, unavailable capture, accessible descriptions, and removal when disabled. It verifies placement at ten page zoom levels from 50% to 200%, unobscured selected values through 100%, and slider thumb clearance at zero volume. The artwork stays unchanged through output, mute, and waiting states. Set `VOLUME_SLIDER_SCREENSHOTS` to save icon and zoom screenshots.

`npm run test:normalization-browser` uses installed Chrome or Edge with synthetic WAV audio and local routes on the platform origins. It verifies actual LUFS worklet frames with no output connections, quiet/loud matching at 30% and 40%, available native headroom, opposite-phase stereo, frequency-dependent correction, stable slider/storage preference, mute preservation, capture cleanup, and ordinary playback after cross-origin capture fails. A same-source quiet-to-loud transition verifies attenuation within 250 ms. The same behavior is exercised with a real Content Security Policy blocking Blob worklet modules and with AudioWorklet removed, verifying native frame processing. With both input APIs absent, playback continues at the selected volume with unavailable status. It never contacts YouTube or Twitch. Set `VOLUME_SLIDER_CHROME` to choose a browser and optionally `VOLUME_SLIDER_SCREENSHOTS` to save options screenshots.

`npm run test:normalization-compare` uses optional FFmpeg reference metering and an optional attributed speech/music corpus. It also processes 30 minutes of sample-clocked audio at 96 kHz without retaining audio or frame history beyond the bounded meter/controller windows. See [testing](testing.md#loudness-comparison) for the method, audio attribution, and reproduction instructions.

These tests do not establish compatibility with every live player version, Twitch stream type, ad transition, or browser. Measured consistency does not replace subjective listening across real content.
