## Added

- **Both:** **Auto-normalize volume** helps keep listening levels more consistent when switching YouTube videos or Twitch channels.
  - The feature starts off. Enable **Volume Slider Options → Audio → Auto-normalize volume** separately on each site; a status below the toggle shows whether it is active, waiting, or unavailable.
  - Your slider continues to set your preferred listening level. Louder sources are reduced promptly, quieter sources rise gradually, and silence or very low background levels do not trigger increases. Automatic corrections leave the custom slider and saved volume preference unchanged.
  - Quiet sources can use the remaining range up to the player's normal 100% maximum. Audio settings show the adjusted output percentage so you can see the correction being applied.
  - A small, static waveform icon tucked into the volume ring's upper-right edge shows when normalization is enabled. It uses the site's accent color, works with both icon styles, and hides when the option is off. It slightly overlaps the outer arc while leaving the volume number clear, and the control keeps its original layout and round background. Hover the icon for an explanation, or open Audio settings for current status and adjusted output.
  - Turning normalization off or setting slider mode to Off restores your chosen volume while preserving mute. If the browser cannot analyze a stream, ordinary playback continues at your chosen level.
  - Audio analysis stays on your device and uses only the playing media. It requires no microphone access and sends no audio anywhere.

## Known limitations

- **Both:** Browser audio capture support and stream restrictions determine availability. After a reload, analysis may wait for a click on the player.
- **Both:** Normalization is approximate and takes time to adapt, so the first moments of a new source and short peaks can still differ in loudness. Increases stop at the player's normal 100% maximum; especially quiet recordings may remain quieter than the target.
